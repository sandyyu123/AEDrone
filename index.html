01How it works

Three steps between collapse and defibrillation

AEDrone removes the search for an AED entirely. Instead of running to find a cabinet, the defibrillator flies a straight-line path to you.

1 t+0s

Tap the emergency button

The app pinpoints your GPS location and submits an emergency AED request with a single confirmation. No forms, no searching.

2 dispatch

The nearest drone launches

A location-aware dispatch layer assigns the closest ready drone, which flies an AED directly to your coordinates.

3 en route

Track it live on the map

Watch the drone approach in real time — distance, status, and arrival — while you stay with the patient the whole time.

04Technology

A three-tier real-time system

AEDrone links a cross-platform mobile client, a real-time cloud backend, and an autonomous flight controller into one pipeline from tap to touchdown.

Flutter client tier 1 · dart

One Dart codebase renders both the public emergency view and an administrative dispatcher dashboard, on iPhone, iPad, Mac, and Apple Vision.

Supabase backend tier 2 · postgresql

PostgreSQL with authentication, row-level security, and a realtime change-feed that pushes every request and drone update in under a second.

Autonomous flight tier 3 · mavsdk / mavlink

A Raspberry-Pi-class companion computer speaks MAVSDK/MAVLink to the autopilot, streams a GPS heartbeat, and releases the AED payload with a servo claw.

✚ AEDrone

AEDrone is a research project exploring drone-delivered defibrillation for out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, built with Flutter, Supabase, and open-source flight control software.

In a real emergency, always call your local emergency number (911 in the US) first. AEDrone is a research prototype and is not a substitute for emergency medical services.
